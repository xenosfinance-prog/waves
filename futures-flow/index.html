Desk read

The dominant theme across yesterday's session was short covering: WTI, natural gas, gold, silver, and the S&P 500 all posted price gains alongside falling open interest, the classic signature of shorts being bought back rather than fresh longs driving the move. WTI told the sharpest version of that story — price up 2.77% while total open interest shed 18.1k contracts, the largest absolute drawdown in the complex, with CLX26 alone accounting for 29.7k of those departing contracts and the CLX26-CLJ27 backwardation spread widening a further 2.00 $/bbl on the session, leaving a curve structure that looks increasingly fragile for any remaining short position. Gold and silver echoed the theme with above-average volume at 1.2x and 1.6x their 20-day averages respectively, suggesting the covering was active rather than passive, though the GCV26-GCH27 contango spread moved 44.0 $/oz on the session, a shift wide enough to warrant watching carry economics. The clearest divergence sat in copper and the Nasdaq: copper fell 1.56% while open interest rose 4.4k contracts — the new-shorts read — making it the one market where positioning is building against the prevailing bullish tone elsewhere, with the largest change concentrated in the deferred HGH27 contract. Brent was the other outlier, its front month barely moved at -0.13% yet open interest contracted 2.0% on below-average volume, a long-liquidation read that stands in quiet contrast to WTI's aggressive covering and leaves the Brent-WTI spread at 8.03 $/bbl as a relationship worth monitoring for further divergence in flow character between the two benchmarks.
