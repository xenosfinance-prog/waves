How to read the oil market

WTI and Brent

WTI (NYMEX, delivered at Cushing) is the U.S. benchmark; Brent (ICE, North Sea) prices most of the world's seaborne crude. They trend together, but local supply, pipelines, shipping and export flows move the spread.

The weekly inventory cycle

The API publishes its estimate on Tuesday at 16:30 ET; the official EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report follows on Wednesday at 10:30 ET (16:30 CET), one day later in weeks with a U.S. holiday. Markets react to the surprise versus consensus, to Cushing, and to products (gasoline, distillates) as a read on demand.

Positioning

The CFTC Commitments of Traders reports positions as of Tuesday and is released on Friday at 15:30 ET. Managed money (funds) is the speculative flow; producers and swap dealers hedge physical exposure.

The futures curve

The spread between the front month and later months is a real-time gauge of physical tightness. A curve moving deeper into backwardation while inventories draw confirms a tight market; a flattening curve with builds warns the opposite.
