EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Global financial markets present a mixed picture as a broad selloff in technology and artificial intelligence stocks pressures equity indices worldwide. Energy prices continue to slide despite mounting regional security threats in key supply choke points, while central bank expectations steer currency markets. Asset classes face headwinds ranging from crypto liquidations to macroeconomic growth revisions in Europe.


MACRO & CENTRAL BANKS

European economic momentum remains subdued as Germany revises its third-quarter GDP growth lower to 0.1% quarter-on-quarter and 1.1% year-on-year, confirming near-stagnation. Markets maintain a cautious stance ahead of incoming US PCE inflation figures, keeping Federal Reserve interest rate policy in sharp focus. Precious metals reflect this environment, with gold hovering near 15-month lows due to persistent rate hike bets. In Asia, fiscal policy concerns ease slightly as Japan plans to avoid issuing fresh deficit bonds to manage borrowing.


EQUITIES

Global equity markets face renewed pressure as doubts regarding artificial intelligence valuations return to trigger heavy selling. Asian stocks lead the downturn, with South Korea's Kospi dropping nearly 6% and Japan's Nikkei falling over 3%. In the United States, tech sentiment deteriorates as Anthropic shares break below their initial public offering price of $83 less than a week after debuting on the Nasdaq. European bourses track Asian and US futures lower, pointing to continued market nervousness across tech sectors.


COMMODITIES & ENERGY

Crude oil prices extend recent losses, with Brent falling below $91 per barrel and WTI settling at $82.05, despite reports of growing tanker disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz. US crude oil inventories rise modestly by 126,000 barrels, according to American Petroleum Institute estimates. Chinese refiners take advantage of lower crude benchmarks to step up overseas purchases and build strategic inventories. For natural gas, Goldman Sachs projects structural demand growth driven by data centers, forecasting 52 gigawatts of new capacity additions by 2028.


CRYPTO

Crypto assets face severe downside pressure as Bitcoin drops below the $60,000 threshold, driven by weak spot demand and forced liquidations. Bitcoin miners move to de-risk balance sheets, with CleanSpark selling 2,102 Bitcoin during a quarter in which it produced 1,708 units. On the institutional side, BlackRock launches a $100 million tokenized money market fund in Hong Kong that accepts stablecoins. Regulatory developments progress in South Korea, where a draft bill promotes bank-led stablecoin issuance while allowing technology firms to hold significant equity stakes.


FOREX & RATES

The US Dollar trades lower against major currencies while the Japanese Yen receives support from strong domestic inflation data. Japan services producer prices jump 4.2% in October, reinforcing market expectations for a December Bank of Japan rate hike and pushing short-dated government bond yields higher. The Australian Dollar trades around 66.4 US cents ahead of inflation data, where a core trimmed mean print of 0.4% or higher is expected to influence Reserve Bank of Australia policy settings.


GEOPOLITICAL RISK

Supply chain and shipping risks remain prominent as Iranian authorities attribute recent mine incidents in the Strait of Hormuz to escalating maritime friction. In Europe, the UK Defence Ministry issues an unusual public warning advising businesses of potential Russian sabotage threats. Meanwhile, the United States advances strategic decoupling efforts, signing $2.45 billion in tungsten contracts to secure non-Chinese supplies ahead of a January 1 Pentagon defense procurement ban.


MARKET OUTLOOK

Traders are focused on upcoming US PCE inflation data over the next 24 to 48 hours to assess the trajectory of Federal Reserve monetary policy. In Australia, the CPI release serves as the primary catalyst for short-dated yields and AUD positioning. Equity markets face critical tests as investors monitor tech sector support levels, while Bitcoin traders watch sub-$60,000 price action for signs of stabilization.
