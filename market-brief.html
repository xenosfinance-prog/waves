EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Markets trade in a broadly mixed tone as geopolitical risk from the Iran war and the Russia-NATO escalation narrative compete with pockets of equity and commodity strength. The dominant theme today is energy supply disruption: Brent crude above $110 per barrel is feeding through to inflation prints, central bank re-pricing, and shipping market stress simultaneously. Traders face a complex cross-asset environment where traditional safe-haven relationships are being tested by concurrent rate hike pressures and geopolitical tail risks.


MACRO & CENTRAL BANKS

UK September CPI printed at 3.5% year-over-year, beating the 3.4% consensus and accelerating sharply from August's 3.1%, while core held in line at 2.8%. The hotter-than-expected headline has fully reinforced a Bank of England November rate hike, which is now almost entirely priced in by markets. French 10-year yields are hovering near levels last seen in 2002, and widening European bond yield spreads are signaling latent fiscal stress beneath the surface calm in equity indices. UK gilt markets are also under pressure following the inflation surprise, pointing to sustained tightening bias across major European central banks.


EQUITIES

Global space stocks rallied sharply on the back of a blowout SpaceX Q3 earnings report, providing a bright spot in an otherwise mixed session for equities. Netflix delivered a Q3 earnings beat on profit but shares fell after the company trimmed two full-year targets and issued a disappointing Q4 revenue and advertising guidance. Asian markets saw notable pressure, with NEXTDC shares plunging 44% after power delays exposed bottlenecks in AI data centre buildout, a sector-wide warning for infrastructure-dependent growth plays. The RBA's Hauser commentary has put a November rate hike firmly in play for Australia, adding macro headwinds to Asia-Pacific equity sentiment.


COMMODITIES & ENERGY

Brent crude trades above $110.30 per barrel and WTI moves in sympathy as Middle East supply fears tied to the Iran war and the Hormuz crisis persist. Goldman Sachs warns of a new oil price shock, citing average global freight rates surging to $20 per barrel from $3 per barrel last year as tanker market stress intensifies. The UK government has approved the Jackdaw North Sea gas field, the first such approval since the Iran war escalated energy prices, though near-term supply relief remains limited. Natural gas has plunged to $2.95, reaching extreme oversold technical levels, while gold inches lower as elevated oil prices and Fed rate expectations weigh on the non-yielding metal.


CRYPTO

Bitcoin faces resistance near the $87,000 to $87,700 zone, with Glassnode identifying late-2025 buyers as key sellers capping upside. US spot Ether ETFs have recorded outflows exceeding $1.08 billion in October so far, the largest monthly outflow of 2026, while Bitcoin ETF inflows have slowed after Monday's spike. On the regulatory front, France has proposed liability rules for crypto exchanges offering DeFi access, and Bitwise along with Hyperliquid have submitted SEC rule change requests covering token trading and airdrops as the public comment period closes. Shinhan and SBI are piloting yen stablecoins for Japan-Korea tourist spending, representing incremental institutional adoption at the margins.


FOREX & RATES

The US dollar holds near a three-week low as geopolitical deal headlines around the Iran conflict weigh on safe-haven demand and swing oil prices simultaneously. The euro holds above 1.1200 against the dollar, though widening European bond yield spreads present a structural warning for sustained euro strength. Sterling climbed on the hot UK CPI print before consolidating, with GBP/USD dynamics now firmly tied to the BOE November hike narrative. The Japanese yen is finding support from strong export data, with Japan recording a 16.7% rise in exports for the 13th consecutive month, reinforcing the BOJ's tightening case.


GEOPOLITICAL RISK

US intelligence reports that President Putin ordered a NATO sabotage campaign, with the Leipzig drone attack described as an opening shot, materially elevating the perceived risk of direct Russia-NATO escalation. This development is structurally supportive for European defence stocks and negative for broader European risk sentiment if governments respond with additional countermeasures. The Iran war continues to be the central market-moving geopolitical event, driving oil above $110, disrupting the Strait of Hormuz, and generating the shipping crisis now flagged by Goldman Sachs. Cost-of-living protests are spreading beyond Europe, adding a social instability dimension to the inflation and energy shock narrative.


MARKET OUTLOOK

The BOE November rate decision is now a near-certainty following today's CPI beat, making any further UK inflation or wage data in the coming weeks a primary market mover for gilts and sterling. Oil price trajectory remains the single most important variable across asset classes, with any ceasefire or Hormuz reopening headlines capable of triggering rapid reversals in energy, inflation expectations, and rate pricing simultaneously. Bitcoin's ability to break or fail at the $87,000 to $87,700 resistance zone will set the tone for crypto sentiment in the near term, with ETF flow data the key daily indicator to monitor. Traders should watch French bond spreads, RBA communication, and any official NATO response to the Putin sabotage report as potential volatility catalysts within the next 24 to 48 hours.
